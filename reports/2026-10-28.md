## Highlights

**Prediction markets are where the new code is.** Of the 17 repositories the scout surfaced in its first 14-day window, 8 target Polymarket, Kalshi or Predict.fun. The section already has two "ccxt for prediction markets" attempts ([PMXT](https://github.com/pmxt-dev/pmxt), [Synpath](https://github.com/Synpath-ai/synpath)), a paper trader, an indexer that puts the whole CLOB history in one DuckDB file ([polyledger](https://github.com/nahrek/polyledger)) and Polymarket's own [agent skills](https://github.com/Polymarket/agent-skills). Binary contracts with a hard resolution date are a far easier target for an LLM than a continuous price series, which explains the concentration.

**Live results are poor, and the people running them say so.** nof1's [Alpha Arena](https://alphaarena.ai/) gave six frontier models $10,000 each on Hyperliquid; four lost 42 to 59 percent in three weeks. The Kalshi follow-up with the same setup lost money for all six. The open reproduction [nof0](https://github.com/wquguru/nof0) lets anyone rerun this. The common failure is not bad forecasts but overtrading and position sizing, which is a systems problem rather than a model problem.

**The risk section is six repositories, and five of them have under 70 stars.** Every execution MCP server in the list will place a real order if asked. The only widely used guardrail-shaped project is [TradeMemory Protocol](https://github.com/mnemox-ai/tradememory-protocol), which is an audit trail rather than a pre-trade gate. [Agent Provost](https://github.com/CharmingSteve/agent-provost) (a compliance firewall in front of the Alpaca MCP server) and [pretrade-risk-controls](https://github.com/zayansalman/pretrade-risk-controls) (kill switch, spend caps, high-water-mark halt) are the right shape but tiny.

**Three of the six incidents are prompt injection or control-plane takeover, not bad trades.** [AIXBT](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/1003) lost 55.5 ETH to prompts queued through its dashboard; [Grok/Bankr](https://giskard.ai/knowledge/how-grok-got-prompt-injected-an-x-user-drained-150-000-from-an-ai-wallet) executed a Morse-encoded message as a transfer; [Lobstar Wilde](https://www.theblock.co/post/390722/ai-agent-created-by-openai-dev-accidentally-sends-entire-memecoin-holdings-to-reply-guy) misread a decimal and sent 5 percent of a token's supply. All three had wallets the agent could move funds from without a second check.

**MCP is the integration layer, and brokers are showing up officially.** Alpaca, Alpha Vantage and Massive (formerly Polygon) all ship first-party MCP servers; Polymarket ships skills. A year ago these were all community wrappers. The data side is nearly solved; the execution side still has one official broker server (Alpaca) and community servers for Interactive Brokers, MetaTrader and ccxt.

**Star farming is common in this niche.** Five of the eight repositories in the first weekly scout had the same profile: one commit, zero forks, no license, around 150 stars on the same day, a Telegram or WeChat contact in the README. The scout now flags that pattern. Treat star counts in trading repositories as a weaker signal than elsewhere on GitHub.

## Opportunities and open questions

These are directions that look under-served from reading the list, not trade ideas.

1. **A pre-trade risk gateway that sits in front of any execution MCP server.** Position limits, daily loss halt, notional caps, allow-lists of instruments, and a kill switch, enforced outside the model's reach. The pieces exist in three small repositories; nobody has packaged it as the thing you put between the agent and the broker. This is the most obvious gap in the whole list.

2. **Hard spend limits for wallet-holding agents.** All three injection incidents would have been bounded by a per-transaction and per-day cap enforced by the wallet layer rather than the prompt. Binance's Agentic Wallet (signing stays with the exchange) is one answer; an open equivalent for self-custody does not appear in the list yet.

3. **Contamination-free evaluation for prediction-market agents.** [StockBench](https://github.com/ChenYXxxx/stockbench) does this for equities with post-2024 data. Prediction markets resolve on known dates, so a point-in-time replay harness (order book plus news as of T) is tractable, and [prediction-market-backtesting](https://github.com/evan-kolberg/prediction-market-backtesting) is a start on top of Nautilus. Whoever ships the reference harness sets the standard everyone else reports against.

4. **The forecast-to-position layer.** The live arenas suggest models forecast acceptably and size positions badly. A library that takes a probability and a confidence and returns a position under Kelly-style or drawdown-constrained rules, independent of the model, would be reused by every agent in the list.

5. **Chinese A-share tooling for non-Chinese readers.** The Chinese section has the most active community projects in the list ([easy-stock](https://github.com/jundizhou/easy-stock) at 1.7k stars, [TradingAgents-CN](https://github.com/hsliuping/TradingAgents-CN), [FinanceMCP](https://github.com/guangxiangdebizi/FinanceMCP)) and almost none of it is documented in English. The data-access problem (Tushare, AkShare, T+1 settlement, price limits) is different enough that it is its own field.

6. **An honest "mode" convention.** Most repositories do not say whether they place real orders by default. This list tags every entry `live`, `paper`, `research` or `library`; if the convention spreads to project READMEs (a one-line badge would do), the next incident report gets shorter.
